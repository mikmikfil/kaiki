Your call

The map now runs on somebody's free tile server, and that is a question before real traffic

Google's keyless map embed stopped working on 9 September — it answers 404 and refuses to be framed — so the meeting-point map moved to OpenStreetMap, which is keyless, free and framed by design. The catch is that the OSM Foundation's tiles are a best-effort donation, and their usage policy asks anyone with real volume to move to a provider that sells them. Fine for a demo and for the first operators; a decision before the hosted pages carry traffic. The alternative is a paid tile provider or Google's keyed Embed API, and it is one line either way — the URL is built in a single method and the security policy reads the origin from a single constant, deliberately.

Estimate

Re-measured on 7 September: forty minutes an issue, and falling

The eight issues measured earlier that day — #87, #88, #89, #101 to #105 — took 28 to 72 minutes each from commit to commit, mean 42, median 40, and that time includes reading the issue, building it, writing its tests, updating the documents and refreshing this page. Every estimate below was cut against that number, which is why the total fell from 43–83 hours to 26–40.

The five since have ranged wider, not narrower: #107 at 15 minutes, #108 at 10, #109 at 25, #110 at 30 — and #111 at an hour and twenty-five. The long one is the informative one. It was the first issue whose acceptance criteria could not be met by writing code against a written spec, because it had to discover what was broken and then fix nine separate things; and it had to build a missing product surface (the sandbox payment page) before it could begin. Treat 40 minutes as the planning figure, expect anything that crosses a new boundary — a browser, a plugin host, a server — to cost two or three times that on its first issue, and read the fast ones as the margin.

Two honest caveats. That rate is for written issues on a codebase whose conventions are settled; M4 to M8 have no issues yet, and writing each one is folded into the figures rather than ignored. And the widget, the WordPress plugin and the deployment each bring a new toolchain — an npm bundle with a size gate, a PHP 8.1 plugin with its own linter, a server with DNS and TLS — so their first issue in each case is costed higher than forty minutes, not lower.

M5 held that shape from end to end. #118 to #122 came in at one hour, an hour and a quarter, three quarters, an hour and an hour; #123 at an hour and a half, which is the top of the band and earned it — it added a database table the data model never had, a queued job, a download route and three test files. Twelve issues, about eleven hours. The pattern from #111 repeats in miniature: the issues that cross into something new cost two or three times the ones that sit inside a settled convention, and the cheap ones are the margin rather than the trend.

What no rate can compress: the CI billing block, a Viva and a Stripe sandbox, a myDATA credential, the lawyer's answer on the ναυλοσύμφωνο, and a real server to deploy onto. Four of those five are yours.
